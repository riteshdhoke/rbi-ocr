## 2. Finding 1 — the Devanagari recognition head is never selected

**Severity: this is the single largest defect the gate found.** It caps Devanagari CER, sets
Devanagari LID accuracy to exactly zero, prevents any Devanagari field from being extracted,
and stops the translation stage from ever firing.

### What the pipeline produces on a clean Devanagari page

| Ground truth | Pipeline output | Label |
|---|---|---|
| `बैंकिंग लोकपाल को शिकायत` | `h cicc  chr` | `Latn`, conf 0.533 |
| `राजेश कुमार शर्मा` | `TT HR 2` | `Latn`, conf 0.591 |
| `पनवेल, महाराष्ट्र` | `y-d, H6RI` | `Latn`, conf 0.530 |
| `9106990984` | `9106990984` | `Latn`, conf 0.999 |

### The same page, same weights, with the Devanagari head selected

| Ground truth | `rec_deva.onnx` output | Label |
|---|---|---|
| `बैंकिंग लोकपाल को शिकायत` | `बैंकिंग लोकपाल को शिकायत` | `Deva`, conf 0.933 |
| `राजेश कुमार शर्मा` | `राजेश कुमार शर्मा` | `Deva`, conf 0.999 |
| `2. पता` | `2. पता` | `Deva`, conf 0.935 |

The weights are fine. `models/ocr/rec_deva.onnx` reads the page essentially perfectly.

### Root cause

`app/stages/s4_ocr.py::_recognise_with_script_check` implements adjudication A-6 as a
bootstrap-then-vote loop:

```python
bootstrap = script_hint or DEFAULT_REC_HEAD_SCRIPT      # DEFAULT_REC_HEAD_SCRIPT == "Latn"
lines = list(provider.ocr_page(page, bootstrap))
decision = identify_script(lines=lines, hint=script_hint, recognised_with=bootstrap)
```

`app/pipeline.py` calls `run_tier1(...)` **without a `script_hint`**, so every page in the
service bootstraps on the Latin head. The vote is then taken over the Latin head's output —
which, on a Devanagari page, is Latin-codepoint noise (`h cicc chr`). It votes `Latn`, it votes
it *confidently*, the `decision.rec_head_script != bootstrap` condition is false, and the
second recognition pass A-6 exists to trigger never runs.

A Devanagari page cannot bootstrap itself into the Devanagari head. The mechanism is
circular: the script is a function of the text, the text is a function of the head, and the
head is unconditionally Latin.

The stage's own docstring states the design honestly — *"pass one uses the hint (or Latin) and
pass two — at most one — uses whatever the vote settled on"* — so this is a composition gap
between WS-E2 and WS-F, not a stage that misbehaves against its contract. It went unnoticed
because every stage test supplies lines that already carry the right script, and because
`complaint_form_deva` and `complaint_letter_mar` still produce *25 lines with plausible
confidences*: the failure is invisible to anything that does not read the text.

### Remedies, cheapest first (not in this lane — flagged for WS-E2/WS-F)

1. **Vote on the pixels, not the transcription.** Any image-space script signal before pass
   one breaks the circularity. Adjudication A-6 already reserves `script_id.onnx` as an
   optional drop-in for exactly this.
2. **Recognise a sample of lines with both heads and pick the higher-confidence transcription.**
   No new artifact; costs one extra pass over a handful of crops, not the page.
3. **Make `identify_script` distrust its own input.** A transcription of mostly 1-3 character
   "words" at 0.5 confidence is evidence the head is wrong, not evidence the script is Latin.
   Cheapest guard, weakest signal.
4. **Pass a per-document `script_hint`.** Correct for a known-language mailbox, useless for
   the general case, and does not fix a mixed inbox.

### What this costs in the numbers below

Every Devanagari cell: CER 0.85-1.00, LID 0.000, `complaint_letter_mar` field F1 0.000,
`complaint_form_deva` field recall 0.152. Translation never fires at all (§5). Reading the
report as "the service is bad at Indic" would be wrong — the service has never been asked.

## 3. Finding 2 — deterministic field F1 misses by a wide margin, mostly downstream of recognition

Aggregate over the deterministic identifier fields (account number, IFSC, UTR/RRN, transaction
reference, amounts, dates, card fragment, bank name):

| | TP | FP | FN | P | R | **F1** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deterministic (validators + lookup) | 156 | 335 | 414 | 0.318 | 0.274 | **0.294** |
| SLM-owned fields | 1 | 11 | 413 | 0.083 | 0.002 | 0.005 *(not measured — §1 row 2)* |

Per field, the useful rows:

| Field | P | R | F1 | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ifsc_code` | 0.732 | 0.578 | **0.646** | The best-performing field. Fixed-format, checksum-shaped, 11 chars — the most OCR-error-tolerant thing on the page, and it still loses 42% of instances. |
| `account_number` | 0.804 | 0.417 | **0.549** | High precision, low recall: when the digits survive OCR the validator is right; often they do not. |
| `transaction_date` | 0.346 | 0.815 | **0.486** | The inverse — the date regex fires readily and over-claims. |
| `bank_name` | 0.181 | 0.278 | **0.219** | Lookup-table matching on degraded text. |

**Precision below recall on three of four fields is the interesting part.** These validators
are not failing to find things; they are finding *the wrong things* in noisy transcriptions.
That is a different remedy from "improve recall" — the deterministic layer needs a confidence
floor or a cross-check before it claims a value, not a looser regex.

**A large share of the 335 false positives is a reference artifact, not an error.** `amount`
(69 FP / 0 GT), `utr_rrn` (61 / 0) and `card_fragment` (24 / 0) have **no ground-truth
instances at all** on this split: the renderers for the types that carry them emit empty
`fields` dicts (Q6). The pipeline extracts a value, the reference has no entry, and every hit
scores as a false positive by construction. Excluding the three zero-reference fields lifts
deterministic precision from 0.318 to roughly 0.46 — still far from target, but the honest
band is "0.29 to 0.46 depending on how you treat Q6", not a single number.

**Invariant 9 holds.** 11 fields failed their validator and all 11 carry `VALIDATOR_FAILED`.
Nothing was silently corrected, and no unvalidated identifier reached the envelope unflagged.
That is PRD metric 4 — *"any that reaches the case system unflagged and wrong is a P1
incident"* — and it passes cleanly. The service is producing *wrong* answers, but it is not
producing *confident* wrong answers, which is the failure mode the design was built to
prevent.

## 4. The quality gate — the strongest result in the report

```
expectation  accept  repair  bounce
clean            42      15       0
degraded         16      74       0
bounce            0       0      30
```

- **No `clean` page bounced.** Zero false bounces is the expensive kind of error avoided — a
  bounced complaint means a citizen is told to rescan a document that was fine.
- **Every `bounce`-profile page bounced.** 30/30, no leakage into the recognition path.
- **`degraded` splits 16 accept / 74 repair**, which is the intended behaviour: `accept` and
  `repair` both satisfy a `degraded` expectation.
- Expectation-satisfied rate **0.915**. The 15 `clean` → `repair` cells are the deduction, and
  a repair on a clean page is cheap.

**On the PRD's 5-15% bounce band:** the measured 16.9% is not comparable to it. That band
describes the rate on *real mail*; this corpus is engineered so that 50 of 295 pages (16.9%)
carry the `bounce` degradation profile. The gate reproduced the corpus's designed rate almost
exactly, which is the strongest evidence available here that the Q3 thresholds are calibrated.
It says nothing about what the rate will be on the RBI mailbox.

**Caveat, and it is the sharpest one in this report:** the Q3 thresholds were calibrated
against this corpus (`docs/decisions-wse1.md` §3) using variants 0-2 — the split this report
scores. This row is the most optimistic number here, and the locked split (§7) is what will
test it honestly.

## 5. Translation, escalation, LID and latency

### Translation (Q7) — the stage never ran

**0 of 503 extracted fields carry a `value_en`, and 0 carry `TRANSLATION_UNAVAILABLE`.** Not
one translation was attempted. This is not an MT failure: Stage 6 masks and translates fields
whose value is in an Indic script, and after Finding 1 there are no such values — every
Devanagari page's fields hold Latin noise. The sentinel-survival rate Q7 asks for cannot be
measured until the Devanagari head is reachable. The WS-F corpus-grounded measurement
(54/54 entities intact, `docs/decisions-wsf.md` D-F-15) remains the best evidence on the
record and this run neither confirms nor contradicts it.

### Escalation

22 documents escalated (12.4%), **precision 1.000, recall 0.167** against the
`quality_or_hand` stand-in reference. Every page the router escalated is one the reference
agrees should have been escalated; the router is far more conservative than the reference,
which flags 132 of 177 documents. The reference is a stand-in with no corpus label behind it
(adjudication A-3, WS-D §8), so **recall against it is not a target and should not be read as
one**. The number that matters — the router's false-negative rate via the shadow sampler — has
n = 3 on this split and Tier 2 was unavailable, so all 3 shadow diffs are empty. Not measured.

`tier2_applied` is **0**: all 22 escalations produced `tier2_failed`, because the Tier-2 VLM
rides the same dead HF router. The escalation *decision* path is exercised and correct; the
escalation *outcome* path is untested by this run.

### Detection

| profile | mean IoU | line recall |
|---|---|---|
| clean | 0.767 | 0.953 |
| light | 0.537 | 0.559 |
| scan | 0.454 | 0.453 |
| heavy | 0.354 | 0.285 |

Detection is strong on `clean` and collapses with degradation. Two known measurement effects
inflate that collapse and **both are recorded as caveats, not as excuses**:

1. **Axis-aligned IoU on rotated ground truth** understates IoU on skewed profiles (WS-D §6).
   Known and documented in the harness.
2. **A coordinate-frame mismatch that was not previously documented.** 118 of 177 documents
   had `SKEW_REPAIRED` applied, so the pipeline recognised a *deskewed* raster and its boxes
   are in the deskewed frame — while the ground truth is in the original frame.
   `OCRPage.rotation_applied_deg` records the angle and **nothing in `app/` ever reads it**
   (verified: the field is written in `s4_ocr.py` and consumed nowhere). The harness does not
   un-rotate either. So on every deskewed page, detection IoU is being measured across two
   different coordinate systems.

   This is raised as **Q11** because the consequence may not be confined to the metric: field
   `Evidence.bbox` is derived from those same line boxes, so a consumer that draws an evidence
   crop on the *original* uploaded image — which is what the review UI is for — would draw it
   rotated. Whether that bites depends on which raster the storage layer serves, which is
   outside this lane. Flagged, not diagnosed.

## 6. The three caveats this corpus always carries

Stated in full because every number above inherits them.

1. **Synthetic handwriting is not handwriting.** Every `hand` cell in the recognition table is
   font-rendered glyph jitter (`corpus/index.json` → `caveats.handwriting`). Those cells
   validate plumbing, routing, batching and throughput. They do **not** validate handwriting
   accuracy, and `Latn|hand|clean` CER 0.255 should not be quoted as a handwriting result. The
   real number needs `iiit-indic-hw-words` and real complaint pages. A further wrinkle from
   WS-D §3: kerning and ligation *across* grapheme clusters is absent on handwritten lines, so
   handwritten Devanagari in particular is easier than reality in one respect and harder in
   another.
2. **The locked split is held out.** Variants 3 and 4 of every type — 118 documents — were
   neither run nor scored. Everything here is a dev-split number, and it is optimistic to
   exactly the degree that the build was tuned against variants 0-2. The quality gate was
   (§4). Publishing a final number means re-running with `--split all --final`, once, on code
   nobody has tuned since.
3. **Four document types have thin field references (Q6).** `receipt`, `email_printout`,
   `cheque` and `photo_evidence` return empty `fields` dicts from their renderers, so their
   field reference comes only from line links, and `gt["fields"]` carries English spellings on
   Devanagari pages. Per-type field F1 for those four is not a measurement of the pipeline —
   see the table in §7.

## 9. What to fix, in priority order

Each item names the lane that owns it. None of them are in WS-H2's lane and none were touched.

1. **Bootstrap the recognition head from something other than "assume Latin"** (WS-E2 /
   WS-F). Single largest defect; unblocks Devanagari CER, Devanagari LID, Devanagari field
   extraction and the entire translation stage in one change. §2 lists four remedies.
2. **Restore SLM credits, then re-run** (owner). Two PRD targets and the whole Q10 question
   are blocked behind it. The run is resumable and costs the OCR only.
3. **Give the four thin renderers real `fields` dicts, and emit `value`/`value_en` pairs**
   (WS-D). Q6 is now measured and material: it distorts both the per-type table and the
   aggregate.
4. **Decide whether evidence boxes must be mapped back to the pre-repair frame** (Q11, WS-E1 /
   WS-G). Affects detection metrics for certain and the review UI possibly.
5. **Add a confidence floor or cross-check to the deterministic extractors** (WS-E3).
   Precision below recall on three of four fields says they over-claim on noisy text; the
   flagging is correct (§3) but a flagged wrong value still costs an officer's time.
6. **Re-run the escalation reference once a real label exists** (WS-E2, relates to Q3/A-3).
   Precision 1.000 against a stand-in is encouraging and not yet meaningful.

## 11. Method, and how to disagree with it

- **The scorer imports nothing from `app/`** (WS-D adjudication A-2), and the predictions are
  committed. Anyone can re-score them, change the escalation reference, change the IoU
  threshold, or write a different scorer entirely, without a model directory or a token.
- **`escalated` in a prediction is the router's decision**, not the envelope's
  `escalation_flag`. `assemble.py` ORs "any field needs review" into that flag, which on this
  run is 43 documents against the router's 22 — the difference between missing PRD target 6
  and meeting it. The envelope's broader flag is carried beside it as `needs_review`. See
  `docs/decisions-wsh2.md` for why.
- **A failed document is scored, not skipped.** It is written with `pages: []` and
  `fields: {}` so its ground-truth lines count as deletions and its fields as misses. There
  were none on this run, but the aggregate would have absorbed them honestly if there had
  been.
- **Recall by source decomposes the total.** The harness measures each mechanism's recall
  against the whole ground truth, not against what that mechanism claimed, so
  `lookup_table` 0.015 + `regex_validator` 0.143 + `slm` 0.001 sums to the overall recall.
  A mechanism cannot score 1.0 by extracting one field well and ignoring the rest.
- **Document quality verdict is the worst page's**, not the first page's. Immaterial on a
  one-page corpus; stated because it will not stay immaterial.
- **Latency is single-threaded and sequential by design.** Parallelising the sweep would have
  roughly halved its wall clock and invalidated PRD target 8.

Decisions, with the reasoning: [`docs/decisions-wsh2.md`](../decisions-wsh2.md).
